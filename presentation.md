## Slide 2 — Introduction: Japan is Different

**Title:** Japan: A High-Context Culture

**Text on slide (bullet points):**
- In Japan, HOW you act is more important than WHAT you say
- Most communication is non-verbal
- Gestures, posture, and silence carry deep meaning

## Slide 3 — Bowing (お辞儀, Ojigi)

**Title:** Bowing — The Core Greeting

**Text on slide:**
| Angle | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| 15°   | Casual hello, passing someone |
| 30°   | Formal greeting, showing respect |
| 45°   | Deep apology or very high respect |

**Additional bullet:**
- The person with lower status bows first and deeper
- Bowing too little = rude, bowing too much = awkward back-and-forth

**Image:** A diagram or illustration showing a person bowing at 3 different angles with labels

---

## Slide 4 — Eye Contact

**Title:** Eye Contact — Less is More

**Text on slide:**
- Direct eye contact = aggression or disrespect
- People look at the neck or chest area during conversation
- Children are taught: "do not stare"

**Contrast box:**
| Japan | Western countries |
|-------|------------------|
| Looking away = respectful | Looking away = dishonest |
| Staring = rude | Eye contact = confident |

**Image:** Two photos side by side — one person avoiding eye contact (Japan style), one making direct eye contact (Western style)

---

## Slide 5 — Nodding & Silence

**Title:** Nodding Doesn't Mean "Yes"

**Text on slide:**
- **Aizuchi (相槌)** — constant small nods and sounds ("hai", "un")
- Means: "I am listening" — NOT "I agree"
- Silence (間, Ma) = respect and careful thinking
- Silence is NOT uncomfortable or awkward in Japan

**Image:** Illustration of two people in conversation with thought bubbles, or a minimalist graphic of "..." representing comfortable silence

**Quote on slide:** *"Silence is also a form of communication"*

---

## Slide 6 — Hand Gestures

**Title:** Hand Gestures — Easy to Get Wrong

**Text on slide (3 columns or cards):**

**"Come here"**
- Japan: palm facing DOWN, fingers wave downward
- West: palm facing UP
- Image: two hand drawings showing both

**"That's me / I"**
- Japan: point finger to YOUR NOSE
- West: point to your chest
- Image: person pointing to nose

**"No / Forbidden"**
- Wave hand in front of face OR cross arms in X shape
- Image: person waving hand in front of face

**"Pointing at someone"**
- Pointing one finger = rude
- Use the whole hand (open palm) instead
- Image: open hand gesture

## Slide 7 — Personal Space & Touch

**Title:** Don't Touch — Personal Space Matters

**Text on slide:**
- Large personal space in public (especially with strangers)
- Touching strangers = almost never happens
- No hugging, kissing on cheek when greeting
- Handshakes with foreigners — okay, but Japanese rarely initiate

**Contrast:**
| Japan | Southern Europe / Latin America |
|-------|---------------------------------|
| No touch | Hugs, kisses, shoulder pats |
| Stay at distance | Stand very close |

**Image:** Diagram showing "comfort zones" or two people standing at a large distance

---

## Slide 8 — Business Card Ritual (名刺交換, Meishi Koukan)

**Title:** Business Cards — A Serious Ritual

**Text on slide:**
- Give AND receive with BOTH hands
- Look at the card carefully — read it, don't just put it away
- Place it on the table in front of you during the meeting
- NEVER write on it, fold it, or put it in your back pocket

**Why it matters:**
- The card = the person
- Disrespecting the card = disrespecting the person

**Image:** Photo of a person handing a business card with both hands, bowing slightly

---

## Slide 9 — Common Misunderstandings

**Title:** Easy Mistakes Foreigners Make

**Text on slide (3 "danger" cards):**

**Smiling**
- In Japan, smiling can mean embarrassment or discomfort
- Not always a sign of happiness

**"Hai" (Yes)**
- Means "I heard you" — not always "I agree"
- Foreigners often think the deal is done — it is not

**Laughing**
- Laughing in an uncomfortable situation = nervous reaction, not joy
- Don't take it personally

**Image:** A confused Western businessman shaking hands thinking it went well, vs Japanese person looking uncertain

## Slide 10 — Comparison Table

**Title:** Japan vs The West — Quick Comparison

**Table:**

| Topic | Japan | Western countries |
|-------|-------|------------------|
| Greeting | Bow | Handshake / hug |
| Eye contact | Avoid | Maintain |
| Touching | Rare | Common |
| Silence | Comfortable, respectful | Awkward |
| Nodding | "I'm listening" | "I agree" |
| Pointing | Whole hand | One finger |
| Smiling | Can mean discomfort | Usually means happy |

## Slide 11 — Why It Matters

**Title:** Why Should We Know This?

**Text on slide:**
- Avoid accidental insults in business meetings
- Travel to Japan without confusion
- Build better relationships with Japanese people
- Shows cultural respect and intelligence

**Real example (text box):**
> A foreign businessman kept making direct eye contact to show confidence. His Japanese partners felt uncomfortable and thought he was aggressive. The deal almost failed — not because of the product, but because of eye contact.

## Slide 12 — Conclusion

**Title:** Key Takeaways

**Text on slide (3 big points):**
1. In Japan, restraint = respect
2. Silence and small gestures carry big meaning
3. Learning body language = learning the culture

**Final quote:**
> *"In Japan, respect is shown through what you don't do — not what you do."*
