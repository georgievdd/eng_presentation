Japan: A High-Context Culture

  • In Japan, HOW you act is more important than WHAT you say
  • Most communication is non-verbal
  • Gestures, posture, and silence carry deep meaning
  • One mistake = unintentional offence

Eye Contact — Less is More

  • Direct eye contact = aggression or disrespect
  • People look at the neck or chest area, not the eyes
  • Children are taught: "do not stare"
🇯🇵 Japan🌍 Western countries
Looking away = respectfulLooking away = dishonest
Staring = rudeEye contact = confident
If you visit Japan and try to show confidence by looking someone in the eyes — they will feel uncomfortable, not impressed.
間

Nodding Doesn't Mean "Yes"

🗣️
Aizuchi · 相槌

Constant nods and "hai" during conversation

✅ Means: "I am listening"
❌ Does NOT mean: "I agree"

🤫
Silence · 間 (Ma)

Silence = respect and careful thinking

In Japan: silence is normal and polite
In the West: silence feels awkward

"Silence is also a form of communication."

Personal Space & Touch

  • Large personal space — especially with strangers
  • Touching strangers = almost never happens
  • No hugging, no kiss on the cheek when greeting
  • Handshakes with foreigners — okay, but Japanese rarely start first
🇯🇵 Japan🌍 Southern Europe / Latin America
No touchingHugs, kisses, shoulder pats
Stay at distanceStand very close
名

Business Cards — A Serious Ritual 名刺交換

  1. Give AND receive with both hands
  2. Read the card carefully — don't just put it away
  3. Place it on the table in front of you during the meeting
  4. NEVER write on it, fold it, or put it in your back pocket
📇
Why it matters

The card = the person

Disrespecting the card
= disrespecting the person

誤

Easy Mistakes Foreigners Make

😊
Smiling

A smile can mean embarrassment or discomfort — not always happiness

🗨️
"Hai" (Yes)

Means "I heard you" — not "I agree"

Foreigners often think the deal is done — it is not

😄
Laughing

In an awkward situation, laughing = nervous reaction, not joy

Don't take it personally

Key Takeaways

  1. In Japan, restraint = respect
